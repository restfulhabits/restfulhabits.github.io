A simple wind-down you can actually keep

Sleep hygiene is the set of daily habits that make it easier for your body to feel ready for rest. It is not a cure, and it does not work the same way for everyone. These are starting points, not a prescription:

  1. Keep a regular sleep and wake window. Going to bed and getting up at roughly the same times, including on weekends, helps your body clock stay steady.
  2. Dim the last hour. Bright overhead lights and fast-scrolling screens can make it harder to feel sleepy. If you use a phone, lower the brightness and avoid stimulating content.
  3. Watch late caffeine, alcohol, and heavy meals. Caffeine can linger for hours. Alcohol may make you drowsy at first and then fragment the second half of the night. A very large meal close to bedtime can also be uncomfortable.
  4. Make the room dark, quiet, and a little cool. Blackout curtains, earplugs or a steady fan, and a comfortable, slightly cool bedroom help many people settle.
  5. Give your mind a landing place. A short written list for tomorrow, a paper book, stretching, or slow breathing can be enough. If you cannot sleep after a while, it is reasonable to get up, keep the lights low, and return to bed when you feel drowsy.

Waking during the night is common. It is not, by itself, proof that something is “broken.” If sleep problems are frequent, severe, or affecting your days, a clinician is the right person to talk to — not a website.
