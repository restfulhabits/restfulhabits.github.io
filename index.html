A bedtime routine you can actually keep

Sleep hygiene is the set of daily habits that make it easier to feel ready for rest. It is not a cure, and it does not work the same way for everyone. Use these as starting points, not a prescription:

  1. Keep a regular sleep and wake window. Going to bed and getting up at roughly the same times, including on weekends, helps your body clock stay steady.
  2. Dim the last hour. Bright overhead lights and fast-scrolling screens can make it harder to feel sleepy. If you use a phone, lower the brightness and skip the stimulating stuff.
  3. Watch late caffeine, alcohol, and heavy meals. Caffeine has a half-life of about 5 to 6 hours and can linger longer, according to CDC / NIOSH. A mid-afternoon cup may still be with you at bedtime. Alcohol can make you drowsy at first and then fragment the second half of the night.
  4. Make the room dark, quiet, and a little cool. Blackout curtains, earplugs or a steady fan, and a slightly cool bedroom help many people settle.
  5. Give your mind a landing place. A short written list for tomorrow, a paper book, stretching, or slow breathing can be enough. If you cannot sleep after a while, get up, keep the lights low, and return to bed when you feel drowsy.

Waking during the night is common. It is not, by itself, proof that something is “broken.” If sleep problems are frequent, severe, or affecting your days, talk with a clinician — not a website. This page is not a treatment for insomnia.
